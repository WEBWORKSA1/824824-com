How to use the shift differential calculator

  1. Enter your shift. Add your Base hourly rate, Shift starts, Shift ends and Unpaid break (min). Overnight shifts work as entered, so 23:00 to 07:00 is an 8-hour shift.
  2. Set the differential windows. Check Evening, Night or both, set each window's From and To times, and choose $ per hour or % of base with an Amount. The defaults are 15:00 to 23:00 at $1.50 an hour and 23:00 to 07:00 at 10%.
  3. Add a weekend premium if it applies. Check Weekend shift and set its type and amount. It's added to every paid hour of the shift.
  4. Choose how hours qualify. Pick Only hours inside a window or Whole shift if most of it is inside, whichever your policy uses.
  5. Read the results. Enter Shifts per week and Weeks worked per year to see Pay for one shift, Base pay, the Differential with hours per window, pay Per week and Differential per year. Copy share link gives you a link to this setup.

How the math works

Paid hours are the time from start to end minus your unpaid break. For each window, the calculator measures how much of the shift falls inside it, including windows that cross midnight.

Since it can't know when you took your break, it removes break time from each window in proportion. A 19:00 to 03:00 shift has 4 hours in each default window. With a 30-minute break, 7.5 of its 8 hours are paid, so each window gets 3.75 paid hours.

Each window pays its qualifying hours × its hourly amount: the flat amount, or the percentage × your base rate. At a $32.00 base, 10% is $3.20 an hour. A weekend premium applies to every paid hour, on top of any window.

With Whole shift if most of it is inside, each window is checked on its own. If half or more of your paid time falls inside it, every paid hour earns that window's rate. If not, none do.

Per week = pay for one shift × shifts per week. Differential per year = the differential for one shift × shifts per week × weeks worked per year.

Example: the default night shift

You earn $32.00 an hour and work 23:00 to 07:00 with a 30-minute unpaid break, so 7.5 hours are paid. All of them fall in the night window at 10%, or $3.20 an hour: 7.5 × $3.20 = $24.00. Base pay is 7.5 × $32.00 = $240.00, so the shift pays $264.00. At 3 shifts a week that's $792.00 a week, and over 50 weeks the differential adds $24.00 × 3 × 50 = $3,600.00.

Example: window hours vs whole shift

Same rates, but you work 19:00 to 07:00 with no unpaid break. Counting only hours inside each window, you earn 4 evening hours × $1.50 = $6.00 plus 8 night hours × $3.20 = $25.60, a $31.60 differential. Under the whole-shift rule, 8 of your 12 hours fall in the night window, so all 12 earn $3.20, for $38.40, and the evening rate drops out because only 4 hours are in that window. Over 150 shifts a year, that's $4,740.00 versus $5,760.00.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the whole shift qualifies. Some policies pay only for hours inside the window. Get the window times and the qualifying rule in writing before you rely on a number.
  • Comparing flat and percentage offers by feel. Divide the flat amount by the percentage to find the break-even base rate: $1.50 ÷ 0.10 = $15.00. Above that rate, the percentage pays more, and it grows with every raise.
  • Stacking premiums your policy doesn't stack. This calculator adds evening, night and weekend premiums together. If your employer pays only the highest one, turn the others off.
  • Forgetting overtime. In the US, night-shift differentials must be included in the regular rate used for overtime (29 CFR 778.207), so a week with both should pay more than 1.5 times your base rate for overtime hours. Check it in the overtime calculator.

Differential rules by place

  • United States: the FLSA doesn't require extra pay for night work; it's a matter of agreement between you and your employer (Department of Labor). Some federal employees get differentials by statute. General Schedule employees earn 10% of basic pay for regularly scheduled work between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m. (5 U.S.C. 5545). VA nurses earn a 10% night differential on every hour of a shift when at least 4 hours fall between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m., and a 25% premium for hours between midnight Friday and midnight Sunday (38 U.S.C. 7453).
  • United Kingdom: the National Minimum Wage applies to night workers, but there is no higher legal night rate (GOV.UK).
  • Australia: penalty rates can apply to late-night and early-morning shifts, weekends and public holidays, set by the award or registered agreement that covers you (Fair Work Ombudsman).
