824824 reads as “8 of 24”: the eight-hour share of the day that workers fought for, and that millions of people now work at night, on weekends and on rotations. We build tools and guides for them, and for the people who build their schedules.

Why we exist

Most calculators assume a nine-to-five. Shift work breaks them: a shift that crosses midnight, a split shift, a 12-hour rotation that swings between 36 and 48 hours a week, a night differential that should change the overtime rate. Those are the cases we start with.

The guides follow the same rule. We explain pay, schedules and sleep in plain words, and link every law, threshold and statistic to the official or primary source so you can check it yourself.

How we work

  • Accuracy first. Calculator math is covered by automated tests, including the worked examples you see in our guides.
  • Sources on the page. Every guide and tool lists its sources and the date it was last updated.
  • Your data stays yours. Calculators run in your browser. We only receive what you choose to send through a form.
  • Corrections. If you spot an error or a rule that has changed, tell us. We fix confirmed errors quickly and note significant corrections on the page.

How 824824 is funded

We keep the tools free with:

  • Advertising, labeled and kept away from the controls you use.
  • Referral fees when a business asks us for a software shortlist and chooses to talk to a provider. Fees never change what we recommend.
  • Sponsorships of tools, the newsletter and contests, always labeled and never with editorial control.
  • Reader support, which funds new tools, contributors and contest prizes.

About the name

The number 824 turns up in surprising places, from numerology and Japanese wordplay to postal codes in Bihar and Wyoming. We use it for one reason: 8 of 24. Read what 824 means around the world.
