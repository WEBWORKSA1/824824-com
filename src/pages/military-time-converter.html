How to use the military time converter

  1. Check Right now at the top for the current time on your device in military format, with the 12-hour time and how to say it.
  2. Under 12-hour to military, type a Time such as 7:30 and choose AM or PM. You get the four-digit military time, how to say it and the same time written with a colon.
  3. Under Military to 12-hour, type four digits such as 0645 into Military time to get the standard time and how to say it. Enter 2400 and the converter shows it as midnight at the end of a day.
  4. To convert a roster, paste times into Convert a list, one per line. Times with AM or PM become military time, and times without become 12-hour time. A question mark flags a line that couldn't be read.
  5. Use the Military time chart to look up every hour from 0000 to 2300, its half-past time and how to say it.

How military time works

Military time is the 24-hour clock written as four digits with no colon. The first two digits are the hour, from 00 to 23, and the last two are the minutes. The day runs from 0000 to 2359, so there's no need for AM or PM. Nursing references such as Open RN describe it as the standard for medication records and note that it reduces AM/PM confusion.

Converting from 12-hour time

  • 1:00 to 11:59 AM: drop the colon and add a leading zero if needed. 6:30 AM is 0630.
  • 12:00 to 12:59 AM: the 12 becomes 00. 12:30 AM is 0030.
  • 12:00 to 12:59 PM: keep the 12. 12:30 PM is 1230.
  • 1:00 to 11:59 PM: add 12 to the hour. 7:30 PM is 1930.

To go the other way, reverse the steps: subtract 12 from hours 13 to 23 and add PM, read 12 as PM, turn 00 into 12 AM, and treat 01 to 11 as AM.

Midnight and noon

Midnight has two labels. 0000 is midnight at the start of a day and 2400 is midnight at the end of one, the convention NIST describes, so 2400 on Friday is the same moment as 0000 on Saturday. NIST also considers 12 a.m. and 12 p.m. ambiguous, because noon is neither before nor after noon, and midnight is both. By common convention, 12 AM is midnight and 12 PM is noon, as timeanddate.com notes.

Example: the two 12 o'clock hours

Half an hour after midnight is 12:30 AM. The 12 becomes 00, so it's written 0030 and said "zero zero thirty." Half an hour after noon is 12:30 PM, which stays 1230 and is said "twelve thirty." Mix them up on a chart or a log and the time is off by 12 hours.

Saying it out loud

The converter shows the common spoken form. On the hour, say "hundred": 0700 is "zero seven hundred hours" and 1000 is "ten hundred," not "one thousand." With minutes, read the hour and the minutes as two numbers, so 1930 is "nineteen thirty" and 0645 is "zero six forty-five." In military use, leading zeros are always spoken and the word "hours" goes at the end, as Today's Military explains, so 1930 becomes "nineteen thirty hours." Aviation is stricter: the Federal Aviation Administration reads each digit separately on the radio, so 0920 is "zero niner two zero."

Tips for shift workers

  • Always write four digits. The converter accepts 645, but 0645 on paper leaves no room for doubt.
  • Add the date after midnight. On a night shift that starts the evening of October 8, anything logged at 0300 happened on October 9. The 24-hour clock guide covers more handover mistakes.
  • Don't do arithmetic on the four digits. Military times aren't ordinary numbers, because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. For shift lengths, use the hours calculator.
  • Watch for zone letters. A time like 1400Z is in Coordinated Universal Time, not your local time. The Z is spoken "Zulu," as timeanddate.com explains. The Right now display always shows your device's local time.
  • Convert to decimal hours for pay. A 1900 to 0730 shift is 12 hours 30 minutes, or 12.50 in decimal hours. The decimal hours converter handles the minutes.

Example: the length of a 0745 to 1915 shift

Subtract the numbers directly and you get 1915 − 0745 = 1170, which isn't a real length of time. Count it instead: 0745 to 1845 is 11 hours, and 1845 to 1915 is 30 minutes more, so the shift lasts 11 hours 30 minutes, or 11.50 decimal hours.
