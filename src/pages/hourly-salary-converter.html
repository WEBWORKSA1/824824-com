How to use the hourly and salary converter

  1. Enter what you're paid. Type the amount under Pay, then choose Hour, Day, Week, Two weeks, Half month, Month or Year under Per.
  2. Set your real week. Enter Hours per week or tap a chip: 36 for three 12s, 37.5, 40, 42 for a 12-hour rotation, 44 or 48. Count straight-time hours only. The rotating shift patterns guide lists average hours for common rotations.
  3. Adjust days and weeks. Open Days, weeks, unpaid time and overtime to set Workdays per week, which sets the daily rate, plus Weeks per year and any Unpaid weeks off.
  4. Add regular overtime. If you routinely work extra hours, enter Regular overtime hours per week and the Overtime multiplier. A With overtime column appears beside base pay.
  5. Read the table. Annual pay appears at the top, with every pay period below it, from hourly to yearly. Copy share link gives you a link to this setup.

How the math works

Everything converts through a yearly figure. Paid weeks are weeks per year minus unpaid weeks off.

  • From hourly: annual = rate × hours per week × paid weeks.
  • From daily: annual = daily pay × workdays per week × paid weeks.
  • From weekly or every two weeks: annual = weekly pay × paid weeks, or the two-week paycheck × paid weeks ÷ 2.
  • From twice a month or monthly: annual = paycheck × 24, or paycheck × 12.
  • Back to hourly: hourly = annual ÷ (hours per week × paid weeks).

Overtime adds overtime hours × hourly rate × multiplier × paid weeks to the annual figure, and the With overtime column scales every period by the same proportion. If you take unpaid weeks, the weekly and two-week figures show pay for a week you work, while the monthly and twice-a-month figures spread the smaller annual total over 12 or 24 paydays.

Why every two weeks isn't twice a month

Every two weeks means 26 paydays a year; twice a month means 24. The same paycheck adds up differently: $2,240 every two weeks is $58,240 a year, but $2,240 twice a month is $53,760. In a year with 26 paydays, two months have three paydays. Monthly pay isn't four weeks of pay either, because an average month is 52 ÷ 12, or about 4.33 weeks.

US overtime rules convert salaries the same way to find an hourly rate. A weekly salary is divided by the hours it's meant to cover, and a monthly salary × 12 ÷ 52 or a semimonthly salary × 24 ÷ 52 gives its weekly equivalent (29 CFR 778.113).

Example: comparing two job offers

Job A pays $31.00 an hour for three 12-hour shifts a week. Job B pays a $60,000 salary for 40 hours a week. Job A comes to $31.00 × 36 × 52 = $58,032 a year. Job B works out to $60,000 ÷ (40 × 52) = $28.85 an hour. Job B pays $1,968 more a year, while Job A pays $2.15 more an hour and takes 208 fewer hours a year.

Example: overtime and unpaid time off

You earn $25.00 an hour for 40 hours a week and usually work 4 overtime hours at time and a half. Base pay is $25.00 × 40 × 52 = $52,000, and overtime adds 4 × $25.00 × 1.5 × 52 = $7,800, for $59,800. Enter 2 under Unpaid weeks off and paid weeks drop to 50: base pay becomes $50,000 and overtime $7,500, for $57,500.

Common mistakes

  • Counting overtime hours twice. If you work 48 hours and 8 are paid as overtime, enter 40 in Hours per week and 8 in Regular overtime hours per week. Entering 48 and 8 counts those 8 hours twice.
  • Multiplying or dividing monthly pay by 4. $4,853.33 a month is $1,120 a week, not $1,213.33.
  • Treating a 42-hour average as straight time. A rotation that averages 42 hours can include weeks over 40, and US federal rules don't allow averaging hours across weeks. Total those weeks in the time card calculator, then enter the average overtime separately.
  • Forgetting unpaid time. Unpaid leave or a seasonal layoff lowers your annual pay. Enter it under Unpaid weeks off.
  • Expecting exactly 26 paychecks every year. Twenty-six two-week periods cover 364 days, so roughly once every 11 years a calendar year has 27 bi-weekly paydays.

Pay and overtime rules by place

  • United States: covered non-exempt employees earn overtime after 40 hours in a workweek, averaging across weeks isn't permitted, and a fixed salary for a regular workweek longer than 40 hours doesn't cover the overtime owed (Fact Sheet #23). Being salaried doesn't settle whether you're exempt; the overtime laws guide explains the tests.
  • Ontario: overtime starts after 44 hours a week, and salaried employees qualify unless an exemption applies (Government of Ontario).
  • Quebec: overtime is based on a standard 40-hour week for most workers (CNESST).
  • United Kingdom: employers don't have to pay for overtime, but your average pay for all hours worked must not fall below the National Minimum Wage (GOV.UK). If you work unpaid extra hours, enter your real hours here to see your true hourly rate.
