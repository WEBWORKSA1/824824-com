Overtime Laws Explained: How Extra Hours Are Paid in Five Countries

Updated October 8, 20268 min readBy the 824824 editorial team

On this page

  1. United States: the federal 40-hour rule
  2. Exempt jobs and the 2026 salary threshold
  3. States with daily overtime
  4. Canada: federal, Ontario and Quebec
  5. United Kingdom: no legal overtime premium
  6. India: double pay under the new Labour Codes
  7. Australia: 38 hours plus reasonable additional hours

Overtime sounds simple: work extra hours, earn extra pay. In practice, the trigger point, the multiplier and even whether any premium is owed depend on your country, your state or province, and how your job is classified. This guide covers the core rules in the US, Canada, the UK, India and Australia, with worked numbers you can check against your own pay.

Important: This guide is general information, not legal advice. Overtime rules have many exceptions and they change, so confirm anything that affects your pay with your jurisdiction's labor authority, your union or a qualified employment adviser.

United States: the federal 40-hour rule

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), covered employees who are not exempt must be paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for every hour worked over 40 in a workweek. The Department of Labor's overtime fact sheet spells out the details that trip people up:

  • The workweek is a fixed, recurring block of 168 hours that can start on any day and at any hour.
  • Each workweek stands alone. Averaging hours over two or more weeks is not allowed, so 30 hours one week does not cancel out 50 the next.
  • The FLSA does not require extra pay for Saturdays, Sundays, holidays or regular days off as such. Those premiums, where they exist, come from policy or contract.
  • You cannot sign away overtime. The requirement cannot be waived by agreement between you and your employer.

Example: a 46-hour week

You earn $20 an hour and work 46 hours. You are owed 40 × $20 = $800 in straight time plus 6 × $30 = $180 in overtime, for $980. If you also earn extras such as a night premium, overtime is based on a blended regular rate, because the regular rate includes all pay for employment apart from specific exclusions. The shift differential guide shows that math.

Exempt jobs and the 2026 salary threshold

Some white-collar employees are exempt from overtime. For the executive, administrative and professional exemptions, a job has to meet three requirements: a duties test, a salary basis test and a salary level test. Being paid a salary does not, by itself, make anyone exempt.

The salary level is where the news has been. A 2024 rule that would have raised the threshold was vacated by a court. In May 2026 the Department of Labor announced a technical amendment that removed the vacated 2024 language and republished the regulations from its 2019 rule. The federal figures in force are:

  • Standard salary level: $684 per week, which works out to $35,568 a year.
  • Highly compensated employees: $107,432 in total annual compensation.
Tip: Some states set higher salary thresholds or stricter tests for exemption. If you are salaried and regularly work past 40 hours, check your state labor department's rules before assuming you are exempt.

States with daily overtime

A few states add daily overtime on top of the federal weekly rule, which matters if you work long shifts. The Department of Labor's state-law summary lists these premium-pay rules:

Example: three 12-hour shifts

You work three 12-hour shifts (36 hours) at $20 an hour. Under the federal rule there is no overtime, so you earn 36 × $20 = $720. In California, hours 9 through 12 of each shift are daily overtime: 24 straight-time hours × $20 = $480 plus 12 overtime hours × $30 = $360, for $840. In Colorado the same week pays $720, because the daily trigger there is more than 12 hours.

Canada: federal, Ontario and Quebec

Provincial employment standards cover most Canadian workplaces. The federal Canada Labour Code applies to federally regulated ones, such as banks and air transport.

Federal (Canada Labour Code)

The federal hours of work page sets standard hours at 8 a day and 40 a week, with overtime paid at least 1.5 times your regular hourly wage. Weekly hours are generally capped at 48. You are also entitled to at least 8 consecutive hours of rest between shifts, your schedule in writing at least 96 hours ahead, and the right to refuse overtime in certain situations to meet family responsibilities.

Ontario

According to Ontario's Employment Standards Act guide, overtime starts after 44 hours in a work week and is paid at 1.5 times the regular rate. It is weekly, not daily, unless your contract or collective agreement says otherwise. You and your employer can agree to average hours over two to four weeks. Managers and supervisors doing managerial or supervisory work do not qualify, and many other jobs have special rules or exemptions.

Quebec

Quebec's labor standards body, the CNESST, uses a standard work week of 40 hours for most workers. Hours beyond it are paid at time and a half, a 50% premium. If you ask, overtime pay can be replaced with paid leave equal to the overtime hours times 1.5.

Example: a 50-hour week at $20

In Ontario: 44 × $20 = $880 plus 6 × $30 = $180, for $1,060. In Quebec: 40 × $20 = $800 plus 10 × $30 = $300, for $1,100. Same hours and base rate, $40 apart.

United Kingdom: no legal overtime premium

UK law does not require employers to pay extra for overtime. Any overtime rate, whether time and a half or plain time, comes from your contract. Two legal limits still apply, both covered in GOV.UK's overtime guidance:

  • Minimum wage averaging. Your average pay across all the hours you work must not fall below the National Minimum Wage. This matters most when overtime is unpaid.
  • The 48-hour average. Under the Working Time Regulations, you usually cannot be forced to work more than 48 hours a week on average. You can opt out, but only in a written agreement you sign. You only have to work overtime at all if your contract says so.

Example: the averaging check

Your contract pays a flat £520 for a 40-hour week, and you work 45 hours with no overtime pay. Your average is £520 ÷ 45 = £11.56 an hour. Compare that figure with the current minimum wage rate for your age on GOV.UK. If it falls short, raise it with your employer.

India: double pay under the new Labour Codes

India's overtime rules changed shape in late 2025, when four Labour Codes became operational on 21 November 2025. One of them, the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, replaces the working-hours rules factories followed under the Factories Act, 1948.

  • The old rule. Under the Factories Act, a worker who worked more than 9 hours in a day or 48 in a week earned twice the ordinary rate of wages for the extra time, as TeamLease RegTech explains.
  • The new rule. The OSH Code keeps the double rate, lowers the daily limit to 8 hours and says a worker cannot be required to work overtime without consent.
  • The rules behind the code. Central rules took effect on 8 May 2026. They limit weekly hours to 48, keep overtime at twice the rate of wages and cap overtime at 144 hours a quarter, according to Nishith Desai Associates.
  • State variation. Central rules apply where the central government is the appropriate government, such as railways, mines and major ports. Elsewhere, state rules apply, so limits and procedures can differ by state.

Example: overtime at double rate

If your wages work out to ₹150 an hour and you work 6 overtime hours in a week, those hours pay 6 × ₹300 = ₹1,800, instead of ₹900 at the ordinary rate.

Australia: 38 hours plus reasonable additional hours

Under the National Employment Standards (NES), an employer must not request or require a full-time employee to work more than 38 hours a week unless the additional hours are reasonable, although an award or enterprise agreement can specify different hours. For part-time and casual employees, the limit is the lower of 38 hours and their ordinary weekly hours. The Fair Work Ombudsman's maximum weekly hours fact sheet lists the factors that decide whether extra hours are reasonable, including:

  • any risk to your health and safety;
  • your personal circumstances, including family responsibilities;
  • the needs of the workplace;
  • whether you receive overtime pay, penalty rates or other compensation for the extra hours.

You may refuse additional hours that are unreasonable. The NES does not set an overtime rate. Overtime and penalty rates come from the modern award or enterprise agreement that covers you, and award- and agreement-free employees are not entitled to a higher rate for overtime under the Fair Work Act.

Questions people ask

Does the FLSA require overtime pay for weekends or holidays?

No. The FLSA requires overtime only for hours worked over 40 in a workweek, not for weekend or holiday work as such. An employer policy or union contract may still pay a premium.

What is the federal overtime salary threshold in 2026?

For most executive, administrative and professional exemptions it is $684 per week, or $35,568 a year. The Department of Labor confirmed this in May 2026 when it formally restored its 2019 regulations, and the highly compensated employee threshold is $107,432 in total annual pay.

Is overtime calculated by the day or by the week?

Federal US law uses the workweek and does not allow averaging across weeks. Some states, including California, Alaska, Nevada and Colorado, add daily overtime rules on top.

Do UK employers have to pay extra for overtime?

No. UK law does not require an overtime premium, so any overtime rate comes from your contract. Your average pay across all hours worked must still not fall below the National Minimum Wage.

Are India's new Labour Codes in force?

Yes. The four Labour Codes became operational on 21 November 2025, and central rules took effect on 8 May 2026. State rules govern many employers, so check the rules your state has notified.

Can I refuse overtime in Australia?

You can refuse additional hours beyond the National Employment Standards limit if they are unreasonable. Factors include risks to your health and safety, your family responsibilities and whether you are paid for the extra hours.
