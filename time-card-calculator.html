    How to use the time card calculator

    1. Set the pay period. Pick the date your pay period starts, then choose 1 week, 2 weeks or Custom. Overtime is counted in seven-day blocks from that date, so start on the first day of your employer's workweek.
    2. Enter each shift. Fill in Time in, Time out and Unpaid break (min) for each day you worked. Press + to add a second shift on the same day, or use Fill weekdays like the first day to copy a regular schedule.
    3. Set pay and rules. Under Pay, overtime and rounding, enter your hourly rate, choose an Overtime rule, and set Round punches to and Overnight shifts count toward to match your employer's policy.
    4. Check the results. You'll see total hours as hours:minutes and decimal, split into regular, overtime and double time, plus gross pay and a weekly table. Warnings flag overlapping shifts, breaks longer than the shift, shifts over 16 hours and short rest between shifts.
    5. Save it. Print or save as PDF for a time card with signature lines, download a CSV, or copy a share link or summary. Your entries stay in this browser.

    How the math works

    Overnight shifts

    When the out time is earlier than the in time, the shift crossed midnight, so 24 hours are added. In minutes after midnight, 22:45 to 07:15 is 435 − 1,365 + 1,440 = 510, or 8:30. Equal in and out times count as a full 24-hour shift.

    By default, a shift counts toward the day it started. Each calendar day (split at midnight) divides the shift and its break in proportion to the time on each side.

    Unpaid breaks

    Break minutes come straight off the shift. Under US federal rules, a meal period is unpaid only if you're completely relieved from duty, and 30 minutes or more is ordinarily long enough (29 CFR 785.19). Short rest breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes count as hours worked (Department of Labor), so leave them out of this column.

    Rounding

    Rounding applies to each punch, not to the total. At 15 minutes, 1 to 7 minutes past a quarter hour round down and 8 to 14 round up. Federal rules accept rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour if, over time, it doesn't leave employees unpaid for time actually worked (29 CFR 785.48). The regulation doesn't name 10-minute rounding.

    Decimal hours and overtime

    Totals are added in minutes and converted once at the end: minutes ÷ 60, so 7:40 is 7.67 hours. Overtime is figured for each seven-day block. Daily rules come first, and hours already counted as daily overtime or double time don't count again toward the weekly threshold. Gross pay is regular hours × rate, plus overtime and double-time hours × rate × their multipliers.

    Example: one night shift, rounded and not

    You punch in at 22:56, out at 07:06, and take a 30-minute unpaid meal. Unrounded, you worked 7:40, or 7.67 hours. With 15-minute rounding, the punches become 23:00 and 07:00, so you're credited with 7:30, or 7.50 hours. Rounding cost 10 minutes here; on other days it may add time, and the federal test looks at the overall result.

    Example: four 12-hour nights under three rules

    Try a night-shift example loads four 12-hour nights (48 hours) at $28.50 an hour if no rate is entered. With each shift counted toward its start day, the US federal preset gives 40 regular and 8 overtime hours: $1,140.00 + $342.00 = $1,482.00. The California preset makes hours 9 through 12 of each shift daily overtime: 32 regular and 16 overtime hours, or $1,596.00. Ontario's preset gives 44 and 4, or $1,425.00. Split the California shifts at midnight instead and the total drops to $1,488.84, because only two calendar days now pass 8 hours.

    Common mistakes

    • Averaging two weeks. Under federal rules, 30 hours one week doesn't offset 50 the next. The 2 weeks setting checks each week separately.
    • Deducting a meal you worked through. If your break was skipped or interrupted, enter 0 for that shift.
    • Reading decimals as minutes. On a pay stub, 7.30 hours means 7 hours 18 minutes. The decimal hours guide has a full conversion table.
    • Paying overtime on base rate when you earn extras. If you also earn a shift differential or a promised bonus, US overtime is based on a regular rate above your hourly rate, so check the week in the overtime calculator.

    Overtime rules by place

    • US federal: time and a half after 40 hours in a workweek, a fixed 168-hour period that can start on any day and hour, with no averaging across weeks (Fact Sheet #23).
    • California: 1.5x after 8 hours in a workday or 40 in a workweek, 2x after 12 in a workday, and on the seventh consecutive workday 1.5x for the first 8 hours and 2x after that (California Labor Commissioner). The calculator applies the seventh-day rule when all seven days of a week are worked.
    • Alaska, Nevada and Colorado: daily overtime after 8 hours (12 in Colorado) plus weekly overtime after 40. Alaska's rule excludes employers with fewer than 4 employees, and Nevada's daily rule covers only employees earning less than 1.5 times the state minimum wage (DOL state summary).
    • Canada: federally regulated workplaces have standard hours of 8 a day and 40 a week, overtime at 1.5x or more, and at least 8 hours of rest between shifts (Government of Canada). Our overtime laws guide covers Ontario and Quebec.
    Important: This is general information, not legal advice. Exemptions, alternative workweeks, averaging agreements and union contracts can change who earns overtime and when, so confirm with your employer, union or labor standards office.

    Questions people ask

    How do I enter a shift that ends after midnight?

    Type the times as worked, such as 19:00 in and 07:30 out. When the out time is earlier than the in time, the calculator treats the shift as overnight, and equal in and out times count as a full 24 hours.

    Is overtime figured on my whole two-week pay period?

    Generally not under US federal rules. Overtime is counted per workweek and hours can't be averaged across weeks, so the calculator splits a two-week period into two seven-day weeks that begin on your pay period start date.

    Should overnight hours count toward the start day or be split at midnight?

    Match your employer's policy or payroll system. Under weekly-only rules the choice matters only when a shift crosses into a new week, but under daily rules such as California's it can move hours between regular, overtime and double time.

    Which rounding setting should I use?

    Use the increment your employer's time clock uses, or No rounding to see your exact time. US federal rules accept rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour only if, over time, it doesn't leave employees unpaid for time actually worked.

    Should I enter my meal break as unpaid?

    Only if you were completely relieved from duty. Under US federal rules a bona fide meal period, ordinarily 30 minutes or more, can be unpaid, but short rest breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes count as hours worked.
